Learning & leadership

Build coastal knowledge through connection and experience

Coastal challenges cross disciplines and communities. UCF Coastal learning initiatives connect students and early-career researchers with research, field experience, communication and collaboration.

Explore the opportunities below. Program details, eligibility, application windows and current activities should be confirmed with the program teams.

Graduate & postdoctoral community

NextGen

Connecting and inspiring the next generation of coastal researchers.

UCF Coastal Next Generation is a community of more than 60 graduate students and postdoctoral researchers, spanning five UCF colleges and 10 academic departments. Members connect, learn, collaborate and build community across disciplines.

Activities described in the program content include monthly research seminars, lab tours, focused discussion groups, multidisciplinary training, field trips and social events. These experiences help members discover new perspectives, develop skills and build collaborations beyond their own research areas.

If you are a UCF graduate student or postdoctoral researcher interested in the coast, contact the center to ask about joining.

Field training initiative

CoastKnights

Ready when the coast changes.

CoastKnights is a UCF Coastal initiative preparing students, faculty and staff to observe, document and respond to changing coastal conditions and extreme events.

Interdisciplinary training and hands-on field experience build capacity to collect time-sensitive observations and turn them into collaborative research that helps us better understand a changing coast.

Undergraduate opportunity

Gulf Scholars

Preparing the next generation of Gulf leaders.

UCF’s Gulf Scholars Program, led by Christopher Emrich, Jennifer Sandoval, Erica Rodriguez Kight and Michelle Taub, helps prepare scientists, engineers, policymakers, communicators and community leaders to understand and respond to challenges facing the Gulf of Mexico and its communities.

The program offers undergraduate students opportunities to work across disciplines and connect their education with real-world issues, including community health, resilience, environmental stewardship, energy, economics and communication.

Program content describes a $575,000 award from the Gulf Research Program of the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, and collaboration with Central Florida Public Media. Confirm the listed leads, award, current funding and partnership details with program owners before publication.
